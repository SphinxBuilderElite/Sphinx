# Part D: Writing, Submission, Acceptance and Conferences

Research KB for Dr Ahmed Atwa (T&O LED, Blackpool). Compiled 21 October 2026.

**What counts for ST3.** The ST3 T&O self-assessment, as briefed, scores only papers indexed in both PubMed and Web of Science with robust peer review. Cureus is excluded by name. First-author papers score highest, and national or international presentations also score. Check the current year's self-assessment guidance before you rely on any "Check" row below, because ESCI status and the treatment of group authorship are exactly where candidates lose points.

**How sure each fact is.** Items marked **[V]** were verified against a source this session (listed in `D_sources.json`). Items marked **~** are approximate bands from recent Clarivate JCR releases. Confirm those in JCR or the Master Journal List (MJL) through your NHS OpenAthens or university login before quoting them anywhere.

## 0. The one-page strategy

- [ ] Every project gets a **target journal and a reporting guideline on day 1**, not after the data are in.
- [ ] Every project gets an **authorship agreement on day 1** (template in section 3.6), with you as first author where you are doing the work.
- [ ] Every audit or service evaluation is checked early. Can it be reframed as a cohort study (STROBE) or as QI (SQUIRE 2.0)? And does it need HRA or REC approval to be publishable as research?
- [ ] Every finished dataset produces **an abstract first** (for the next BOA, specialist society or EFORT deadline) and then **a paper**, submitted within about 8 weeks of the abstract.
- [ ] Target **ST3-eligible journals only** (MEDLINE plus SCIE/SSCI, Yes in the table). Use "Check" journals only after you have confirmed their status in the MJL.
- [ ] Keep a submissions tracker (section 6.6) with dates, decisions and next-cascade journal.

## 1. Reporting guidelines (EQUATOR Network)

### 1.1 Which guideline when

| Study design | Guideline | Current version | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Randomised trial (report) | **CONSORT 2025** | Published April 2025 in BMJ, Lancet, JAMA, Nat Med, PLoS Med [V] | 30-item checklist plus flow diagram. Adds an open-science section (registration, protocol, data sharing, funding/COI) and integrates harms, outcomes and non-pharmacological extensions. Use CONSORT 2025 now; CONSORT 2010 is superseded. |
| Trial protocol | **SPIRIT 2025** | Published 2025 alongside CONSORT 2025 [V] | 34 items, harmonised with CONSORT 2025. Includes patient and public involvement and open science. |
| Observational: cohort, case-control, cross-sectional | **STROBE** | 2007 (no full update yet). STROBE-Equity extension exists [V] | Covers most ortho retrospective series, audits written as cohorts, and registry work. |
| Routinely collected data (EHR, registries, NJR, HES) | **RECORD** | 2015, an extension of STROBE | Use with STROBE for NJR/NHFD/HES work. Report code lists and data linkage. |
| Observational study emulating a trial | **TARGET** | 2025 [V] | Use for "target trial emulation" registry analyses. |
| Diagnostic accuracy | **STARD 2015** | 30 items | Imaging, clinical tests (e.g., scaphoid MRI vs CT). |
| Prediction model, including AI/ML | **TRIPOD+AI** | BMJ 2024 | Replaces TRIPOD 2015 for both regression and ML models. Use for fracture-risk, PROM-prediction and AI imaging models. |
| Systematic review / MA | **PRISMA 2020** | 27 items plus PRISMA 2020 flow diagram | Also PRISMA-S (search), PRISMA-P (protocol), PRISMA-ScR (scoping). Register on PROSPERO before screening. |
| Case report | **CARE** | 2013 checklist | Note that BJJ rejects case reports without review [V]. They rarely score. |
| Quality improvement | **SQUIRE 2.0** | 2015 | An audit cycle with an intervention is QI. Target BMJ Open Quality, Annals RCS or BJO. |
| Online / e-survey | **CHERRIES** | 2004 (JMIR) | Pair with a validated questionnaire design. Report response rate and the denominator. |
| Qualitative | **COREQ** (interviews/focus groups) or **SRQR** (broader qualitative) | 2007 / 2014 | Med-ed and patient-experience work. |
| Economic evaluation | **CHEERS 2022** | 28 items | |
| Animal / lab | **ARRIVE 2.0** | 2020 | BJJ asks for the ARRIVE checklist as a supplement [V]. |
| Using AI in the manuscript | Follow **ICMJE Jan 2026 Section V** plus the journal's AI policy | [V] | See 2.9. |

### 1.2 Checklist workflow

- [ ] Download the checklist from equator-network.org at protocol stage. Paste the items as **comments into your manuscript skeleton**.
- [ ] Fill the "page/line number" column as you write, not at submission.
- [ ] Upload the completed checklist as a supplementary file. Most ortho journals ask, and BJJ requires the matching guideline [V].
- [ ] Use the guideline's flow diagram (CONSORT/PRISMA/STROBE-style patient flow) as **Figure 1**.
- [ ] Put the guideline name in the abstract Methods and in the final sentence of the Methods introduction ("This study is reported according to STROBE").

## 2. Writing method

### 2.1 Fast drafting order (the order you write, not the order you read)

1. **Outline** (one page): question (PICO/PECO), primary outcome, key message in one sentence, target journal and word limit.
2. **Figures and tables first.** Figure 1 is the flow diagram. Table 1 is baseline characteristics. Table 2 is the primary outcome. Then a figure for the key finding. If the tables don't tell the story, writing won't save it.
3. **Results.** Describe the tables in the same order, with no interpretation.
4. **Methods.** Reproducible enough that a registrar in another trust could repeat it.
5. **Discussion.** Five-paragraph skeleton (2.6).
6. **Introduction.** Three-paragraph funnel (2.4), written last because now you know what you found.
7. **Abstract.** Built from the finished sections, matching the journal's headings.
8. **Title.** Last, because it should state the design and the finding.

**Timeline target for a retrospective cohort or audit paper:** tables in week 1, full draft in weeks 2–3, co-author round in weeks 4–5, submission in week 6.

### 2.2 Writing sprints

- [ ] Book 2 × 90-minute blocks per week (e.g., post-nights rest day plus a weekend morning). Put them in the calendar as theatre lists.
- [ ] One sprint, one section. Don't edit and draft in the same sprint.
- [ ] Start each sprint by writing the paragraph's **topic sentence list** (one line per paragraph), then fill in.
- [ ] End each sprint by writing the very next sentence you would have written, so you never restart cold.
- [ ] Monthly "shut up and write" with co-investigators (in person or a Teams call, cameras on, 25/5 pomodoros).

### 2.3 Title and abstract

**Title.**
- [ ] Include the design: "...: a retrospective cohort study", "...: a systematic review and meta-analysis".
- [ ] Include the population and the key variable. Avoid questions and cute titles in BJJ/Injury.
- [ ] Keep it to about 15–20 words. Check whether the journal wants a running title.

**Structured abstract.**
- [ ] Match the journal's headings exactly. BJJ uses **Aims / Methods / Results / Conclusion, max 300 words** [V]. Many Elsevier and Springer journals use Background / Methods / Results / Conclusions at 250 words.
- [ ] In Methods, state the design, setting, dates, n, primary outcome and analysis.
- [ ] In Results, give **numbers with effect sizes and 95% CIs**, not just p values.
- [ ] Make the conclusion a direct answer to the aim, with no new claims. Add a "level of evidence" line if the journal asks for one (JBJS, AOTS).
- [ ] For keywords, give 4–6 terms, using **MeSH** terms (check the MeSH Browser at meshb.nlm.nih.gov) plus 1–2 free-text terms not in the title.
- [ ] Add "Article focus / Key messages / Strengths and limitations" boxes where the journal requires them (BMJ Open, BJJ-family journals).

### 2.4 Introduction: the funnel (3 paragraphs, about 350–500 words)

1. **The problem.** How common or costly it is (UK figures: NHFD, NJR, GIRFT), in one or two sentences with citations.
2. **What's known and the gap.** Summarise the best evidence (latest SR, RCT, guideline such as BOAST or NICE), then state exactly what is unknown or contradictory.
3. **Aim.** "The aim of this study was to..." plus the primary hypothesis or outcome. Stop there. Don't preview results.

### 2.5 Methods: reproducibility checklist

- [ ] **Design and reporting guideline** sentence.
- [ ] **Setting**: trust, type of unit, dates of data collection and of follow-up.
- [ ] **Ethics**: REC/HRA reference, or audit/service-evaluation registration number plus the trust's statement that REC wasn't required. Note the consent arrangement.
- [ ] **Participants**: inclusion/exclusion criteria and how cases were identified (coding, theatre logs, PACS).
- [ ] **Interventions/exposures**: enough detail to replicate (implant, approach, rehab protocol).
- [ ] **Outcomes**: primary outcome defined in advance, secondary outcomes, and the PROM instrument and its version.
- [ ] **Data sources and measurement**: who collected the data, whether blinded, and inter-observer reliability if radiographic.
- [ ] **Bias**: how confounding was addressed.
- [ ] **Sample size**: either a power calculation, or "all consecutive eligible patients" plus a precision justification.
- [ ] **Statistics**: tests, software and version, handling of missing data, adjustment, and significance threshold. Name who did the analysis.
- [ ] **Patient and public involvement** statement (BMJ journals require one).
- [ ] **Registration**: RCTs **before first participant consent** (ICMJE Jan 2026) [V]; SRs on PROSPERO.

### 2.6 Results, then Discussion

**Results.**
- [ ] First paragraph: flow (screened, included, lost to follow-up), referring to Figure 1.
- [ ] Baseline (Table 1), then primary outcome, then secondary outcomes, then subgroup or sensitivity analyses.
- [ ] Don't repeat every table number in the text. Highlight the key ones.
- [ ] Report absolute numbers with percentages, effect estimates with 95% CI, and exact p values.

**Discussion (5 paragraphs, about 1,000–1,200 words).**
1. **Principal findings.** Two or three sentences answering the aim.
2. **Comparison with the literature.** Where you agree and disagree, and why that might be (population, technique, follow-up).
3. **Strengths and limitations.** Be honest and specific: retrospective design, single centre, missing PROMs, selection bias. Say what effect each probably had.
4. **Implications.** For clinicians, for services (BOAST/NICE compliance) and for research (what the next study should be).
5. **Conclusion.** One short paragraph with no new data and no "more research is needed" filler. Say what research, specifically.

### 2.7 Using AI ethically for language editing

The ICMJE recommendations (January 2026 update, new Section V) say [V]:
- [ ] **Disclose AI use at submission** (cover letter and/or methods or acknowledgements), describing what the tool did.
- [ ] **Verify everything** the tool produces for accuracy, completeness and bias. Never cite AI output as a source.
- [ ] **AI cannot be an author.**
- [ ] **Never upload confidential material** (patient data, unpublished co-author drafts without consent, manuscripts you are reviewing) to an AI tool that doesn't guarantee confidentiality.
- [ ] Acceptable uses: grammar and clarity on text you wrote, structure critique, checklist cross-checking, code debugging (and say so).
- [ ] High-risk uses: generating references (hallucination risk), writing the Discussion from scratch, image creation or alteration (forbidden for data figures).
- [ ] Check each journal's own AI policy. BJJ points to a separate AI policy page [V].

Suggested disclosure: *"During preparation of this manuscript the authors used [tool, version] to improve the readability and language of author-written text. The authors reviewed and edited all output and take full responsibility for the content."*

### 2.8 Common reasons for desk rejection in ortho journals

- [ ] Out of scope, or the wrong article type (BJJ has stopped publishing case reports and rejects them without review [V]).
- [ ] Over the word, figure or **author limit** (BJJ: 4,000 words including abstract and references, 8 tables, 10 figures, max 8 authors [V]).
- [ ] **Previously posted preprint** at journals that refuse them (BJJ will not consider papers posted to preprint servers [V]. JBJS, CORR and JOR signed the same 2019 joint policy for clinical research [V], so check their current stance).
- [ ] No new message: a small single-centre retrospective series repeating known findings.
- [ ] No ethics statement, an unregistered RCT, or missing reporting checklist.
- [ ] Audit framed as research without the appropriate approvals.
- [ ] Overstated conclusions, causal language from observational data, p-value-only reporting.
- [ ] Poor English or formatting (fix this before submission, not after).
- [ ] Similarity-check (iThenticate) overlap with your own earlier abstract or thesis. Cite it and rephrase.
- [ ] Salami slicing (a second paper from the same cohort with no clear new question).

```
Response: We agree. ...
Change: ...

R1.2 **[Comment]**
Response (respectful disagreement): We appreciate this point. However, [reason with citation/data]. We have acknowledged this as a limitation (page X, lines Y-Z).

REVIEWER 2

```

Rules:
- [ ] Respond to **every** comment, including the compliments ("Thank you").
- [ ] Number the comments (R1.1, R1.2...) so the editor can audit them in two minutes.
- [ ] Quote the changed text. Don't make the editor hunt for it.
- [ ] Disagree with evidence, never with tone. When a comment is impossible (e.g., "add 5-year follow-up"), say so and add it as a limitation.
- [ ] Re-run the word count and the reporting checklist page numbers.
- [ ] Return a **major revision within 4–6 weeks** (most journals give 30–60 days, so ask for an extension early if needed) and a minor revision within 1–2 weeks.

### 2.11 Typical timelines (submission to publication)

| Stage | Typical ortho journal |
|---|---|
| Technical check / desk decision | 1–14 days |
| First decision after review | 4–8 weeks (BJJ: 6–8 weeks [V]) |
| Revision by authors | 2–8 weeks |
| Second decision | 2–6 weeks |
| Acceptance to online first | 2–8 weeks (BJJ: 3–4 months to publication [V]) |
| **Realistic total** | **4–9 months** for an accepted paper. Add 2–4 months per rejection cascade step. |

**Implication for ST3:** a paper is usually counted once accepted or published and indexed (check the current rules). So submit by about **12 months before** the portfolio upload date to allow one rejection.

## 3. Authorship and credit

### 3.1 ICMJE criteria (all four required)
1. Substantial contribution to conception or design, **or** to acquisition, analysis or interpretation of data; **and**
2. Drafting the work or revising it critically for important intellectual content; **and**
3. Final approval of the version to be published; **and**
4. Agreement to be accountable for all aspects of the work.

People who don't meet all four go in the **Acknowledgements** (with their permission). AI tools cannot be authors (ICMJE Jan 2026) [V]. The January 2026 update also requires at least one academically affiliated author to have **direct access to the primary data** in collaborative or industry studies [V].

### 3.2 CRediT taxonomy (14 roles)
Conceptualization; Data curation; Formal analysis; Funding acquisition; Investigation; Methodology; Project administration; Resources; Software; Supervision; Validation; Visualization; Writing – original draft; Writing – review & editing. Elsevier, Springer, Wiley and Bone & Joint journals capture these at submission, so assign them at project start.

### 3.3 Group and collaborative authorship and ST3
- Collaborative (e.g., "XYZ Collaborative", national audits, trainee research networks) papers list a **group name as author**, with individuals listed in an appendix or as "collaborators" in PubMed.
- **Why the ST3 scoring discounts them:** you aren't the named first author. PubMed indexes you as a collaborator (in a separate field), not as an author, and the scoring rewards demonstrable leadership of the writing. Collaborator status usually scores at the lowest tier or not at all. Check the current guidance.
- **Action:** join collaboratives for skills and networking, but negotiate **a writing-committee position** (named author) or a **spin-off first-author paper** from local data (with steering-committee permission).

### 3.4 Corresponding author duties
- [ ] Handles submission and all correspondence. Ensures every author has approved each version.
- [ ] Collects COI forms, CRediT statements, ethics documents and the data-sharing statement.
- [ ] Gets all authors to sign off on any change of authorship after submission (BJJ requires a letter signed by all authors for changes after peer review [V]).
- [ ] Responds to post-publication queries, corrections and data requests for years afterwards.
- [ ] Being corresponding author signals leadership. Take it when you are first author, unless a supervisor needs it for institutional OA funding (a Jisc-deal eligibility issue, see 4.2).

### 3.5 Conflicts of interest and ORCID
- [ ] Every author completes the **ICMJE Disclosure Form** (most journals accept it). Declare industry education grants, course sponsorship, consultancy, and non-financial interests (e.g., author of a guideline you evaluate).
- [ ] **ORCID**: register (free), link it to Scopus, Web of Science (Researcher Profile) and Google Scholar, and allow trusted organisations (Crossref) to auto-update. Use the same name format on every paper (e.g., "Atwa A" or "Atwa AN"; pick one forever).

### 3.7 COPE and disputes
- COPE flowcharts cover "changes in authorship" before and after publication, "ghost/guest/gift authorship" and "how to spot authorship problems". Journals follow them. You can't win a dispute at the journal stage that you didn't document at the start.
- [ ] Keep emails and an authorship log (date, decision, who agreed).
- [ ] For escalation within the trust: research and development office or the clinical audit department, then the medical director. Universities have research integrity officers.

## 4. Journal selection for orthopaedics

### 4.1 How to choose
- [ ] Is it **ST3-eligible**: MEDLINE/PubMed **and** WoS (SCIE/SSCI)? Confirm in the **NLM Catalog** (search "currentlyindexed") and the **Clarivate Master Journal List** (mjl.clarivate.com).
- [ ] Does the article type match (does the journal take SR/MA, technical notes, retrospective cohorts)?
- [ ] Is the scope matched? Have they published similar papers in the last 2 years? Cite them.
- [ ] Do the word, author and figure limits fit (BJJ max 8 authors [V])?
- [ ] What is the **preprint policy**?
- [ ] Is there an APC or deal coverage (4.2)?
- [ ] Speed: check "time to first decision" on the journal's insights page (Elsevier and Springer show it).
- [ ] Cascade: which sister OA journal does it transfer to?

**Rejection cascade examples**
- General ortho: **BJJ → JBJS OA / BJO → AOTS / Int Orthop → EJOST / JOSR**
- Trauma: **Injury → JOT → AOTS → EJOST**
- Arthroplasty: **J Arthroplasty → BJO / Hip Int / Knee → Arthroplasty Today (check status)**
- Shoulder: **JSES → JSES International / Shoulder & Elbow (check ESCI) → Int Orthop**
- Hand: **JHS Eur → JHS Am → Hand Surg Rehab (check) → BMC MSK**
- Med-ed: **Medical Education → Medical Teacher → J Surg Educ → BMC Med Educ → Clinical Teacher (check ESCI)**
- SR/MA: **EFORT Open Reviews → JBJS Reviews → BJO → BMC MSK/JOSR**

### 4.2 UK open-access deals and APC waivers
- **No central NHS fund for APCs.** NHS Knowledge and Library Services state "There is not a central NHS pot for APC charges". Most NHS publisher contracts are read-only subscriptions, and APCs are typically funded from grants (NIHR, Wellcome) or study leave budgets [V].
- **Jisc read-and-publish (transitional) agreements cover UK university-affiliated corresponding authors**, not NHS-only affiliations. Recent examples: **Elsevier–Jisc 3-year R&P agreement announced 23 Feb 2026** [V]. Wiley, Springer Nature, SAGE, Taylor & Francis, OUP and BMJ also have Jisc agreements (BMJ–Jisc OA agreement [V]; check each in **Jisc Open Policy Finder** or the ESAC registry).
- **Practical route for you.** Your **University of Warwick** student affiliation (MSc Med Ed via iheed) may or may not make you eligible as a corresponding author. Ask Warwick Library's open-access team whether postgraduate (taught/distance) students qualify. Otherwise, make a university-affiliated co-author (e.g., a Lancaster/Manchester/Liverpool honorary clinical academic) the corresponding author, and agree this in the authorship agreement.
- **Waivers.** Many OA journals offer waivers or discounts (Research4Life countries, hardship). BJO states a waiver policy [V]. Ask before submission, not after acceptance.
- **Green OA is free.** Self-archive the accepted manuscript (BJJ: 12-month embargo [V]). This doesn't affect ST3 scoring but helps citations.
- **Hybrid journals don't require an APC.** Publish closed access at no cost (BJJ, Injury, JOT, AOTS...), which is fine for ST3.

## 5. Predatory journals and research integrity

### 5.1 Think. Check. Submit. (thinkchecksubmit.org)
- [ ] Do you or your colleagues know the journal? Have you read its articles?
- [ ] Can you identify and contact the publisher (a real address, not just a webform)?
- [ ] Is the peer-review process clear, with realistic timelines?
- [ ] Is it indexed in the databases you use? (Verify in the **NLM Catalog** and **Clarivate MJL** yourself. Don't trust the journal's own claims.)
- [ ] Are the APCs clear and stated before submission?
- [ ] Is the editorial board real (do the members list it on their own institutional pages)?
- [ ] Is the publisher a member of COPE, OASPA or DOAJ (for OA)?

### 5.2 Red flags (Beall-style)
- Unsolicited flattering email ("Dear Esteemed Author... your eminent work...").
- Promises of peer review in under a week or guaranteed acceptance.
- Fake or "invented" metrics (e.g., "Global Impact Factor", "Index Copernicus value" presented as an IF).
- A journal title mimicking a real one, a scope covering "all of medicine and engineering", or a board with no ortho expertise.
- An APC demanded after acceptance, with no prior mention.
- Claims of "indexed in PubMed" that are actually PMC deposits of individual NIH-funded papers, or Google Scholar listing.

### 5.3 Hijacked journals
- Clone websites copy a legitimate journal's title and ISSN. Use the **Retraction Watch Hijacked Journal Checker** (Abalkina and Retraction Watch; a dynamic Google Sheet with a methods page) [V].
- [ ] Always reach the journal through the **publisher's own site** or the link in the **MJL/NLM Catalog**, never through an email link.
- [ ] **DOAJ** (doaj.org) lists vetted OA journals, e.g., BJO with its APC and waiver info [V]. Not being in DOAJ isn't proof of predation, but being in it is reassuring.

### 5.4 Integrity pitfalls
- **Duplicate or redundant publication**: the same data published twice without cross-reference. Conference abstracts don't count as prior publication, but say so in the cover letter.
- **Salami slicing**: splitting one study into minimal publishable units. Editors check for overlapping cohorts, so always cite the related paper.
- **Image manipulation**: never adjust radiographs or figures beyond uniform brightness and contrast. Anonymise without altering the anatomy.
- **Citation manipulation**: coercive citation by reviewers or editors, and citation cartels. Self-citation only where relevant.
- **Paper mills and fabricated data**: high-volume OA journals are under scrutiny. Keep raw data and audit trails (BJJ can ask for raw data [V]).
- **Plagiarism/self-plagiarism**: run your own similarity check if possible and rewrite rather than reuse the methods text verbatim.
- **Retractions and corrections**: ICMJE Jan 2026 dropped "replacement" in favour of "retraction with republication" for honest major errors [V].

### 5.5 Preprints (medRxiv)
- **BMJ journals**: preprints are allowed (BMJ is a medRxiv founding partner with direct transfer). Case reports are excluded [V].
- **BJJ: will not consider papers posted to preprint servers** [V]. The 2019 joint editorial of CORR, BJJ, JOR and JBJS said they would not accept previously preprinted clinical research [V], so check JBJS, CORR and JOR's current pages.
- [ ] Rule: **decide on the target journal before posting a preprint.** For ortho clinical research, the default is **don't preprint** unless the target journal explicitly allows it.

## 6. Submission mechanics

### 6.1 Systems
- **Editorial Manager** (Elsevier, Springer, Wolters Kluwer: Injury, AOTS, JOT, Spine...), **ScholarOne Manuscripts** (SAGE, Wiley, T&F, BJJ-family), **Snapp/Springer Nature submission**, and **BMJ ScholarOne**.
- [ ] Have these ready before you log in: title page (authors, affiliations, ORCIDs, corresponding author), blinded manuscript, tables, figures (TIFF/EPS at ≥300 dpi; line art ≥600–1,000 dpi), reporting checklist, ethics letter, COI forms, cover letter, CRediT statement, data-availability statement, funding statement, and highlights or key messages if required.
- [ ] Blinded review: remove trust names from the manuscript and figures, and redact the hospital name from the ethics text. Supply it separately.
- [ ] Expect a "technical check" bounce for formatting. Fix it within 48 hours.

### 6.2 Journal finder tools
- **JANE** (jane.biosemantics.org): paste your abstract and it suggests journals and similar authors (possible reviewers).
- **Elsevier JournalFinder**, **Springer Nature Journal Suggester**, **Wiley Journal Finder**, **SAGE Journal Recommender**, **T&F Journal Suggester**.
- [ ] Cross-check every suggestion against the MJL and NLM Catalog for ST3 eligibility.

### 6.3 Cascades and transfers
- Many publishers offer **transfer** with reviews attached (e.g., JBJS to JBJS OA; BJJ to BJO; JSES to JSES International; Elsevier's Article Transfer Service). This saves weeks.
- [ ] Accept a transfer only if the receiving journal is ST3-eligible (check ESCI status).

### 6.4 After acceptance
- [ ] Sign the licence (CC BY if funder-mandated; otherwise the journal default).
- [ ] **Proofs**: you usually get 48–72 hours. Check author names and order, affiliations, numbers in tables, figure legends and references. Don't rewrite at this stage.
- [ ] **Embargoes**: respect press embargoes (BMJ, Lancet). Don't tweet before the online date.
- [ ] Deposit the accepted manuscript (green OA) where allowed. Update ORCID.

### 6.5 Promoting the paper and tracking citations
- [ ] **ORCID** auto-update via Crossref; **Google Scholar profile** (public, verified NHS/university email); **Web of Science Researcher Profile** (ex-Publons), which also records your peer review.
- [ ] Post a figure and a one-line takeaway on **X / LinkedIn / Bluesky**, tagging the journal, BOA, BOTA and the specialist society. Many journals (BJJ, JBJS) amplify posts.
- [ ] Ask the journal about an **infographic** or a podcast (BJJ offers infographics for accepted papers for a nominal fee [V]).
- [ ] Present at the next regional or national meeting. Add it to the portfolio (ISCP/eLogbook evidence).
- [ ] Set **Google Scholar alerts** for citations and track them via Scopus/WoS citation reports.
- [ ] Become a **reviewer** (register on Web of Science Reviewer Recognition, then email editors in your niche). It's good CV evidence and good writing training.

## 7. Conferences (ST3 presentation points)

### 7.1 What to know
- **National or international podium and poster** presentations usually score. Regional meetings usually don't. Check whether the current ST3 guidance weights podium over poster and first-presenter over co-author.
- **Orthopaedic Proceedings** (Bone & Joint Publishing) publishes **abstracts of papers presented** at participating societies' meetings. **Posters are not included** [V]. These are abstracts, not peer-reviewed papers, so they don't count toward publications. They also don't block later full publication, though you should mention them in the cover letter.
- **Typical costs**: registration £150–£450 for trainee or member rates (BOA, BHS, BASK), €300–€700 for EFORT/SICOT. Add travel and accommodation. Study leave budget usually covers a presenting author. Apply early.
- Abstract fees: most UK societies don't charge to submit, but the presenting author **must register**.

### 7.3 Turning an audit or SR into an abstract (300 words)
- [ ] **Title**: design plus population plus key finding ("Compliance with BOAST distal radius standards before and after a departmental pathway: a two-cycle audit").
- [ ] **Introduction/Aim (2 sentences)**: the standard (BOAST, NICE NG38) and the aim.
- [ ] **Methods (3–4 sentences)**: design, dates, setting, n, standard and target (e.g., ≥90%), intervention between cycles, analysis.
- [ ] **Results (4–5 sentences)**: give numbers. Cycle 1 versus cycle 2 compliance for each standard (with percentages and p or 95% CI). For an SR: studies, patients, pooled effect (95% CI), I².
- [ ] **Conclusion (1–2 sentences)**: what changed and what you'll do next.
- [ ] Avoid "results will be presented" (it gets rejected) and abbreviations without definitions.
- [ ] Match the submission category (trauma, hand, education, QI). Audit and QI often fit **education/governance** or **Best of the Best** streams better than science sessions.
- [ ] One dataset can lead to **one national abstract plus one international abstract**, as long as you avoid duplicate presentation within the same region and season. Check each meeting's rules on previously presented work.
