## 0. The one-page version

1. **Publishing a lot is a pipeline problem, not a willpower problem.** Prolific clinician-researchers run many projects at staggered stages, so something is always being submitted. One project at a time gives you one paper every 12 to 18 months. Eight staggered projects give you one submission every 6 to 8 weeks.
2. **ST3 scoring is capped. Your long-term ambition is not.** The T&O self-assessment scores your best 2 first-author PubMed papers (2 points each, max 4) and your best 2 other papers (1 each, max 2). Your NIHR Associate PI role on FACT.1 is accepted as one of the two "other" items [S1, S2]. The points therefore need only about **2 strong first-author papers and 1 more other paper**. Everything beyond that builds your interview, FRCS-era CV and lifelong habit. Design for both: secure the points first, then build volume.
3. **Exclusions matter.** Published abstracts, case reports, letters and technical tips do not count. Papers must be PubMed-indexed, and the 2027 guidance (secondary source) also requires Web of Science indexing. Cureus is given as an example of a journal that does not count, and it was delisted from Web of Science in 2025 [S1, S2, S27]. Collaborative group names do not count as first authorship [S2].
4. **Daily-writing dogma is weaker than people claim, but scheduled writing works.** Boice's famous studies were small, closely coached and short-term. Sword's survey of more than 1,300 academics found that only about 12% write every weekday [S5]. The robust lesson is to **schedule** writing, protect it, track it and make it social. "Every single day" is optional [S4, S5, S6].
5. **Most presented work never gets published.** Only 23.7% of UK hip and knee society abstracts reached full publication [S12]. Your rule: **no project gets presented unless a manuscript draft exists.**
6. **A team multiplies you, if you lead it properly.** Students and FY doctors join for authorship and learning. They stay if roles are clear, authorship is agreed in writing up front, and meetings are short and regular [S8, S9, S10].
7. **Integrity is a career asset.** Honorary authorship affects about 42% of orthopaedic papers by ICMJE criteria [S14]. Paper mills target exactly the "publish in mass" mindset [S16]. Since January 2026, ICMJE requires AI use to be disclosed [S15]. One retraction costs more than ten papers gain.

## 1. What prolific clinician-researchers actually do (experience-based advice, curated)

### 1.1 Recurring patterns among high-output surgeon-researchers

| Pattern | What it looks like in practice | Evidence or source |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduled, non-negotiable writing time | Fixed blocks treated like a theatre list. Silvia suggests about 4 hours a week, and regularity matters more than total hours. | Silvia, *How to Write a Lot* [S4] |
| Reject "specious barriers" | "I need to read more first" or "I need a free weekend" are excuses. Use writing blocks for any writing-adjacent task: analysis, proofs, tables. | Silvia [S4] |
| Accountability and social writing | Writing groups, Writing Accountability Groups (WAGs) and Shut Up and Write sessions. These make writing "automatic, mechanical and routine". | Skarupski, Johns Hopkins WAGs [S7]; Shut Up and Write health professions education groups [S6] |
| Flexible routines, not one dogma | Successful academics write at varied times and places. Some binge-write. What they share is behavioural, artisanal, social and emotional habits (Sword's "BASE"). | Sword, "Write every day: a mantra dismantled" [S5] |
| Collaboration and scale | Trainee collaboratives produce large, multicentre datasets that no single doctor could gather. UK T&O national trainee collaboratives went from 0 starts in 2019 to 10 between March 2020 and June 2021. | Khaleeq et al. 2022 [S11]; JTO June 2026 spotlight [S10] |
| Points-based contribution systems | Oxford Trauma trials award collaborator status for 10 or more points from defined tasks (setup, consent, training colleagues, reporting complications), plus ICMJE writing and approval criteria. | WHiTE 9 collaborator agreement [S9]; Oxford Trauma trainee involvement [S19] |
| Protected time and support staff | When surgeons are surveyed, the top barriers to output are lack of time and lack of research assistants. Prolific people solve both by delegating to a team. | Alaseem et al., *World J Orthop* 2026 [S20] |
| Finishing | Studies with significant results and stronger designs are the ones that reach print [S12]. Prolific authors finish and resubmit fast. | Collier et al., *BMJ EBM* 2018 [S12] |

### 1.2 Realistic numbers (calibrate your ambition)

- **US orthopaedic residents (2020 entry)** averaged 3.8 publications during medical school. More than 150 of 688 had none, and about 10 had more than 30. Much of what applicants list is abstracts rather than full papers [S13]. Heavily skewed distributions are normal.
- **Systematic reviews** take a mean of **67 weeks** from registration to publication, with a mean team of 5 [S17]. Do not plan a solo SR as a "quick win".
- **Abstract-to-publication conversion** in UK orthopaedic meetings is 23.7% [S12]. Beat this by drafting the manuscript before the abstract deadline.
- **A realistic stretch target for a full-rota LED with a team:** 8 to 12 PubMed and WoS-indexed outputs over 30 months, 3 to 5 of them first-author, plus 4 to 6 national or international presentations. A solid baseline is 5 to 6 outputs with 2 to 3 first-author. Anything near 20 a year without protected time should make you suspicious of the method (see section 6).

### 1.3 Pitfalls and failures, with the countermeasure for each

- [ ] **Too many idea-stage projects, none finished.** Countermeasure: WIP limits (section 2).
- [ ] **Abstract presented, paper never written.** Countermeasure: the abstract goes in only after a full draft exists.
- [ ] **Waiting on a consultant's comments for months.** Countermeasure: 2-week deadlines in writing, a polite chaser at day 10, and agree up front that "silence after 3 weeks = approval to submit" (record it in the authorship agreement).
- [ ] **Rejection paralysis.** Countermeasure: name the next 2 target journals at the start; resubmit within 14 days.
- [ ] **Data governance trouble** (a retrospective review published without the right approval). Countermeasure: classify every project as audit, service evaluation or research using the HRA decision tool, and register it with the Trust's audit or R&D office before collecting data.
- [ ] **Student team drift.** Countermeasure: one task per person with a due date, visible on a board, and fortnightly 30-minute stand-ups.
- [ ] **Predatory or delisted journals.** Countermeasure: check the NLM Catalog (MEDLINE or PMC) and the Clarivate Master Journal List before choosing a journal. Use Think.Check.Submit.
- [ ] **Collaborative "authorship" that does not count.** Countermeasure: secure a defined role (writing group, regional lead, data analysis) and documentary evidence of it [S2, S8].

### 1.4 UK trainee and IMG lessons, distilled

- Recruiting to NIHR portfolio trials earns a certificate per recruit, and Associate PI status earns a scored portfolio item. Register **before** you start, because retrospective recognition is not allowed. You can hold only one Associate PI role at a time, for at least 6 months [S3, S19].
- Medical-education papers are a legitimate early route. Tomlinson's 2024 guide covers why to publish, what, where and how [S21]. You have a structural advantage here through your Warwick programme.
- Regional collaboratives (CORNET, BON, SWORD and a North West Orthopaedic Research Collaborative) offer committee roles, including foundation and core trainee representatives. These provide leadership evidence and named roles [S10, S22].

## 2. The pipeline model

### 2.1 Stages (use these exact column names on your board)

| # | Stage | Exit criterion (what "done" means) | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Idea bank | One-line question plus feasibility note | n/a |
| 1 | Scoped | PICO or question, design, target journal, senior author agreed, authorship agreement signed | 1 to 2 weeks |
| 2 | Approvals | Audit/R&D/HRA registration done, PROSPERO or OSF registration if SR, data access confirmed | 2 to 8 weeks |
| 3 | Data | Dataset locked, with a second person validating 10% | 4 to 12 weeks |
| 4 | Analysis | Tables and figures final, statistician signed off | 2 to 4 weeks |
| 5 | Writing | Full draft circulated with a 2-week deadline | 3 to 6 weeks |
| 6 | Submitted | Confirmation email filed | n/a |
| 7 | Revision | Response submitted within 21 days of decision | 2 to 4 weeks |
| 8 | Accepted or published | PMID recorded, evidence pack updated (section 7) | n/a |

### 2.2 WIP limits (the rule that makes this sustainable)

- **Lead (first or senior author) projects active in stages 1 to 7: maximum 4.**
- **In stage 5 (Writing) where you are the writer: maximum 2.**
- **Contributor projects** (collaboratives, colleagues' studies, FACT.1 outputs): maximum 4, each with a defined task.
- **Total active: 6 to 8.** A new project enters stage 1 only when one leaves stage 5.
- Rule of thumb: always have **1 project in writing, 1 in analysis, 1 in data, and 1 under review.**

## 3. Building and leading a research team

### 3.1 Where to recruit

- **Medical students:** University of Liverpool lists Blackpool Teaching Hospitals as a specialist clinical teaching site [S23]. Lancaster places students mainly at the Royal Lancaster Infirmary and Furness General [S24]. UCLan (Preston) and Edge Hill (Ormskirk) also have medical schools nearby. Confirm through Blackpool's undergraduate medical education office which cohorts rotate through T&O. Ask the student surgical and orthopaedic societies to advertise a "Research Apprenticeship" for 12 months.
- **FY1 and FY2 doctors and core trainees** on the T&O rota. They need portfolio items too.
- **Physician associates, ANPs, physiotherapists, hand therapists, orthogeriatric nurses:** often the best data owners (for example hand therapy for trapeziectomy outcomes, and NHFD nurses).
- **Senior authors:** Mr Charalambous (educational supervisor), the audit leads (Mr Khan, Mr Bilal) and the FACT.1 local PI.
- **Statistician:** the Trust R&D office, an NIHR Research Support Service, or a university partner (Lancaster or Liverpool). Offer co-authorship for real analytic input.
- **Librarian:** the NHS knowledge and library service at Blackpool, for SR search strategies. Acknowledge them, or include them as an author if they meet ICMJE criteria.
- **International:** Ain Shams colleagues for SR screening, cross-cultural surveys and Arabic PROM validation.

### 3.2 Role cards (copy one per person into the onboarding pack)

**Principal lead (you)**
- Owns the question, the board, the authorship agreement and the final draft. Chairs the stand-up. Allocates tasks. Has the final say on journal choice with the senior author.
- Time: 4 to 6 h a week.

**Senior author (consultant)**
- Clinical sense-check, approvals sponsor, critical revision, accountability.
- Asks: a 20-minute meeting at stage 1, and 2-week turnaround on drafts.

**Project deputy (FY or senior student)**
- Runs fortnightly stand-ups when you are on nights. Chases tasks. Maintains the data dictionary.
- Earns: a high author position and leadership evidence.

**Data collectors (students or FY)**
- Collect data to the data dictionary in REDCap or a locked spreadsheet. Log queries. Expect a 10% double-check.
- Earn: co-authorship if they also meet ICMJE drafting, revision and approval criteria, otherwise an acknowledgement or collaborator listing.

**Data validator (independent)**
- Re-extracts a 10% random sample and reports the error rate.

**Analyst or statistician**
- Builds the analysis plan before data lock. Runs the analysis or reviews yours.

**SR screeners (2 or more, independent)**
- Title and abstract, then full-text screening in Rayyan or Covidence. Resolve conflicts by consensus or a third reviewer.

**Librarian**
- Search strategy, translation across databases and the PRISMA-S reporting section.

**Writing group (2 to 3 people)**
- Draft sections against the reporting guideline (STROBE, PRISMA, SQUIRE, CHERRIES and so on).

### 3.3 Authorship agreement (sign at stage 1)

- [ ] Use ICMJE's 4 criteria as the bar for authorship, with CRediT roles to describe who did what [S15, S26].
- [ ] Provisional author order and the rule for changing it (contribution-based, reviewed at data lock).
- [ ] Deadlines for each role. If a task is missed by more than 4 weeks without notice, the role is reassigned and the person moves to acknowledgement.
- [ ] Senior author turnaround: 2 weeks. Silence after 3 weeks counts as consent to submit.
- [ ] Data ownership, storage location, and who keeps access when people leave.
- [ ] Spin-off rule: secondary papers need group agreement. No salami slicing.
- [ ] AI use policy: disclosed, human-verified, no confidential data in public tools [S15].
- [ ] For collaborative-group papers, follow the National Research Collaborative and ASiT role framework, and ask the journal to make collaborators PubMed-citable [S8].

### 3.6 Keeping volunteers motivated

- Give **visible, finishable tasks** (for example "extract 20 records by Friday").
- Give a **certificate of contribution** at each milestone. Students use these in their portfolios and foundation applications.
- **Teach while doing.** The STARSurg evaluation found that students' confidence in protocol writing, data collection and approaching clinical staff rose significantly [S8b].
- **Rotate presenting:** a student presents the poster or local talk, and you keep the podium for your scored ones. Note that each project counts once for presentations.
- Write a **reference letter** for anyone who delivers.
- Remove people kindly but promptly when they go silent. That protects the ones who deliver.

## 6. Integrity and reputation

### 6.1 Hard rules
- [ ] **No paper mills or bought authorship.** Treat any unsolicited offer of "authorship slots" or "guaranteed publication" as fraud [S16].
- [ ] **No honorary or gift authorship**, including automatic listing of department heads (OR 5.03 for honorary authorship when that happens) [S14]. Acknowledge instead.
- [ ] **No salami slicing.** One dataset with one primary question gets one paper. Secondary papers must ask a genuinely different question and cite the primary paper [S28].
- [ ] **AI:** disclose its use in the manuscript and cover letter, verify every claim and reference, never list AI as an author, and never paste patient data or confidential manuscripts into public tools (ICMJE, January 2026) [S15].
- [ ] **Register before you collect** (PROSPERO, OSF, ClinicalTrials.gov or ISRCTN where relevant). Write the analysis plan before data lock.
- [ ] **Data governance:** HRA decision tool, Trust registration, Caldicott principles, anonymisation.

### 6.2 Profile building
- [ ] ORCID iD linked to every submission. Let Crossref auto-update it.
- [ ] Google Scholar profile (public, institutional email verified). Merge duplicates.
- [ ] ResearchGate and LinkedIn: post accepted-manuscript versions only where the publisher allows.
- [ ] Consistent author name: "Atwa A" or "Atwa AN". Choose one and never change it.
- [ ] **h-index:** it grows mostly from being part of cited, multicentre and review work over 5 to 10 years. Do not optimise for it now. Optimise for papers people cite (SRs, multicentre cohorts, useful surveys).
- [ ] Peer review for 2 to 3 journals a year (Web of Science reviewer profile). Good for learning and editor relationships.

| Cycle time B | Days from rejection to resubmission | Under 14 days |
| Review turnaround | Days a draft sits with co-authors | Under 21 days |
| Acceptance rate | Acceptances divided by decisions | Tracked, not targeted |
| Team throughput | Tasks closed per member per fortnight | 1 or more |
| ST3 points secured | Live score on Q6 to Q12 with evidence attached | Rising each quarter |

### 7.3 Gamification (light-touch)
- XP per stage transition (10), submission (50), acceptance (100), podium (75), resubmission within 14 days (bonus 25).
- Streaks counted in **weeks**, not days, so night shifts do not break them. This follows Sword's caution against guilt-based regimes [S5].
- A quarterly "boss fight": the hardest stalled task gets a dedicated 3-hour block.
- A team leaderboard for tasks closed only (never for authorship counts).

- [ ] Sunday: plan (15 min)

- [ ] Quarterly: retrospective plus mock self-assessment with your educational supervisor
- [ ] Annually: update your CV, ORCID and Scholar profiles, and write a one-page "research narrative" (useful for interviews)
